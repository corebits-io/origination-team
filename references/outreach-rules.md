# Outreach rules

The system drafts. A human sends, by hand, after reading every word. That
line is not configurable, and most of the rules below exist because of what
happens when it blurs.

---

## The ceiling

- **Nothing is ever sent by this system.** No email, no LinkedIn, no letters,
  no connection requests. Drafting and queueing is the ceiling
- **No contact details are found or guessed.** Drafts are addressed to a
  person at a company. How the letter physically reaches them is the user's
  job, with their own tools and their own legal review
- **Volume is capped by the buy box.** Section 10 says how many first touches
  a week the user will send. The queue never holds more than two weeks of
  that number in `drafted` status. A hundred queued letters is not a
  pipeline, it is a mail merge wearing a nicer shirt

---

## What a draft is built from

Only from cells in the row, each of which carries evidence:

- The owner's name and evidenced role
- The company's segment, place, and anything the company says about itself
- The signal, quoted or paraphrased *without exceeding the quote*
- The buyer's identity and credibility line from buy box section 9

If a draft needs a fact that is `Not found`, the draft works around it or the
row is returned to the analyst. The writer never fills a gap with something
plausible. Personalization built on invented facts is the error that reaches
a human — the exact human you most wanted to impress.

---

## What a draft never contains

- **A claim the evidence does not carry.** The signal says "proud to watch
  the next generation take our quality system forward"; the draft may say
  "clearly proud of what the next generation is building". It may not say
  "as you plan your exit"
- **Anything from a person's private life.** See
  `references/owner-research-rules.md`
- **A valuation, a multiple, or "businesses like yours are worth..."**
- **False scarcity or deadlines.** No "we are finalizing our list this month"
- **"We have buyers for businesses like yours"** — this system is for
  principals buying for themselves, and the intake gate enforced that
- **A pretended prior relationship.** "Following your company for years" is
  out unless the buyer actually has, and says how

## What a draft always contains

- Who the buyer actually is, in one sentence, verifiable from their site
- Why this company specifically — the personalization hook, traceable to a
  cell in the row
- A small, honest ask: a conversation, not a transaction
- An easy no: one line that makes declining comfortable. Owners talk to
  buyers who make saying no easy

---

## Tone

Plain English. Short sentences. No "synergies", no "exciting opportunity",
no exclamation marks. Written to be read aloud by one person to another. If a
sentence would sound odd spoken across a shop counter, rewrite it.

Letters run 120-180 words. LinkedIn notes run under the platform limit with
room to spare. Every draft ends with the sender's real name and role from buy
box section 9.

---

## Legal note, plainly

Outreach to business owners touches real law: CAN-SPAM and state equivalents
for email, GDPR and PECR in Europe, telemarketing rules for phone. This
package does not send anything partly *because* compliance depends on
channel, jurisdiction, and the user's own status. The drafts are copy, not
legal advice. Have your counsel look at your outreach practice once; it is
cheap compared to the alternative.
